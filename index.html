Agricultural Survey Drone

Designed and built a quadcopter capable of 22-minute survey flights and a 3.5 kg payload for about $2,200, giving small farmers a survey tool they otherwise couldn't afford.

22 minsurvey flight time
3.5 kgpayload capacity
~$2,200total cost, ~$4,000 under competitors
3.8 mhorizontal survey accuracy
Why

Commercial agricultural drones are often out of reach for small farms. A medium-size chassis can meet the flight time and payload needed for a range of specialized survey tasks, giving smaller farmers access to critical information about their land.

How

I paired a 650 mm carbon fiber chassis with 340 KV motors, 15 × 5.5 in propellers, and an 8000 mAh battery, producing 9 kg of thrust at 6,000 RPM for the 2.9 kg airframe. Designing to a 1.5 factor of safety for loaded missions allowed a 3.5 kg payload. A custom ESC and power distribution board configuration handles current spikes up to 200 A, giving ample headroom for survey missions.

Result

The powertrain delivered stable 22-minute unloaded survey runs with enough thrust to lift 3.5 kg. Autonomous survey missions achieved roughly 3.8 m horizontal accuracy, sufficient for surveys at 100 ft altitude, at a total cost about $4,000 below comparable drones.

Thermal Survey Camera & Mapping Pipeline

Built a Raspberry Pi–based thermal imaging payload and data pipeline that turns drone survey footage into a color-coded temperature map, helping orchard farmers spot irrigation and sunburn problem areas.

20×22 cmground area per thermal pixel
~5%temperature accuracy
100 ftsurvey altitude
70%image overlap
Why

Farmers benefit from a low-cost way to find heat-related problems across an orchard. The project also served as the first real survey test of the agricultural drone.

How

A Raspberry Pi connects to the flight controller over UART, where a C program reads time-stamped MAVLink position data. Frames are captured simultaneously from a PureThermal 3 board with a FLIR Lepton 3.5 in Y16 format. Surveys fly at 100 ft and 6 ft/s with 70% overlap, chosen from the camera's frame rate and field of view. A Python script matches each frame's timestamp to its position, stitches the frames into a thermal map, and converts it into a color-coded map of local average temperatures. The Pi is powered onboard by a 5 V UBEC from the power distribution board, and I designed and 3D printed the camera enclosure, pivoting boom arm, and Pi case.

Result

A low-cost, portable thermal imaging solution. With each thermal pixel covering roughly 20 × 22 cm, the system resolves average temperatures across an orchard block to about 5% accuracy, and the scripts compile the frames into a map overlaid on a 2D base map.

Citywide Bike Route Plan

Wrote and presented a bike infrastructure report for the City of Wenatchee's engineering staff. The city used it to install new signage and replace missing signs, with positive feedback from local cyclists.

114existing signs mapped
37missing signs identified
4new routes proposed
6routes marked for improvement
Why

Following community outreach about the city's deteriorating bike routes, the city engineer asked for an overview of existing infrastructure and options for adding community-requested routes, building on a previous local transportation board report.

How

Using recent Google Street View imagery and field surveys, I mapped every existing and missing bike route sign, updating a 10-year-old, unfinished tracking database of sign locations and conditions. Using that data along with safety, equity, and accessibility criteria, I recommended new routes and improvements, then compiled everything into a report and location map.

Result

The city used the updated database to install new signage and replace missing signs, making the bike network more accessible and earning positive feedback from local cyclists.
